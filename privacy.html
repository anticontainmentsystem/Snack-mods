Privacy

How Snack’s desktop app and built-in Email connector handle information.

Who to contact

The publisher account for this testing project is anticontainmentsystem. Questions about Snack or this policy can be sent to anticontainment.system@gmail.com.

Account access you choose

Connecting email opens your provider’s sign-in and permission screen. Gmail access uses the read-only and send scopes: reading discovers and retrieves command emails; sending returns receipts and results. Outlook and Zoho request the account information and mail read/send access needed for the same workflow. Other mailboxes use the IMAP/SMTP credentials you supply. Authorizing access does not automatically permit every email sender to control Snack.

What the Email connector processes

Snack checks the connected mailbox for new messages while the app runs. Depending on the provider API, it receives message identifiers, sender addresses, subjects, timestamps and message content while finding command emails. It checks the allowed-sender list and a private command code before executing a command. It ignores its own automatic replies.

Command results may contain workspace information, positions, source information or other data returned by the action you requested. Results and receipts are sent to the command sender through the connected mail provider. Choose allowed senders and action permissions with that in mind. The connector does not automatically interpret natural-language mail or forward all incoming mail to an AI service.

Where information is kept

The current desktop Email implementation connects directly from your computer to your provider. Its access/refresh tokens, mailbox passwords and private command codes are kept in Snack’s local Vault using operating-system encryption. Connection settings, allowed senders, access settings and command history are kept in the app’s local storage. Command history includes message identifiers, sender/recipient, action, execution status, returned results or errors, and reply acceptance information. These records remain until you remove that mailbox connection or remove the app data.

The Email connector does not send those credentials or command history to a Snack publisher-operated email relay. Mail providers still process the authorization requests, mailbox requests and sent messages according to their own policies. Emails already sent remain in the provider’s systems according to the applicable account settings.

Your controls and deletion

Serving Tray → Connect → Email lets you pause checking, change allowed senders, adjust permitted action areas, or disconnect a mailbox. Disconnect removes that connection’s locally stored credentials, private command code and local command records. It does not delete messages from your provider’s mailbox. You can also revoke Snack’s authorization from the provider’s connected-app settings.

Agents, sharing and Condiments

Other connections are separate from email authorization. If you use an agent, a remote connection, a tunnel, or an optional third-party Condiment, information made available to it depends on that feature and its permissions. A permitted action can produce data which is then included in an email result. Review these connections and the third party’s own information before enabling them. This policy does not promise that independently authored Condiments have identical data handling.

Google API data

Snack’s use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. The built-in Email connector does not sell mailbox data, use it for advertising, or train AI models on it. It uses the authorized data to provide the user-facing email command, receipt and result features described above.
